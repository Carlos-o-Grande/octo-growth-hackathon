## 2. Fee benchmark: is €150 per integration realistic?

All sources are third-party marketing pages, read on 8 Nov 2026. Ranges are in US dollars.

| Source (third-party) | Nano tier | Micro tier | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Hubfluence, "2026 Rate Benchmarks"](https://www.hubfluence.io/youtube-influencer-rates) | "Nano (1K to 10K subs)": "$100 to $500" | "Micro (10K to 100K subs)": "$500 to $3,000" | Integration CPM "roughly $20 to $50 per 1,000 expected views for a 60 to 90 second integration" |
| [Collabpals rate calculator](https://www.collabpals.com/tools/youtube-sponsorship-rate-calculator) | "Nano (1K-10K)": integration "$50 - $500" | "Micro (10K-50K)": integration "$200 - $2,000"; "Mid-Tier (50K-500K)": "$1,000 - $10,000" | Formula: "Average Views / 1,000 x Niche CPM x Format Multiplier x Engagement Multiplier" |

**Verdict.** €150 is realistic for **nano creators and small micro creators whose videos get a few thousand views**. It sits inside the nano ranges and at or below the floor of the micro ranges. At the $20 to $50 CPM above, €150 buys roughly 3,500 to 8,600 expected views (at an *assumed* €1 = $1.15), which matches the plan's 5,000 views per integration. It is **not realistic** for Layla at ProcessDriven, Tim Harris AI, Jasper Tech, Be Productive or James Blue. With 40K to 150K subscribers and 10K to 400K views on relevant videos, they sit in the $500 to $3,000+ bands. For them, offer free access for their team and ask for their rate for a later flight.

Recommendation for the two paid slots:
1. **Joseph Chandler** (9.76K; his note-taker video reached 53,959 views; he already takes paid promotions).
2. **Solopreneur** (26.9K; relevant video at 3,329 views, so €150 matches the CPM math). Backup: Be Productive.

To make €150 easier to accept, offer **€150 flat plus €10 per trial sign-up from their link, capped at €25 per creator**. Worst case is 2 x €175 = €350, exactly the channel budget. The team decides.

## 4. Integration script (45 to 60 seconds, creator adapts)

Uses only the "can" column of the brief. Spoken word count: 131 words (counted with code), about 50 to 55 seconds at a normal pace. Creators should film Octo on **their own** call after setup. Our 36-second demo video needs Oleksii's OK before anyone uses it.

| Time | Beat | On screen | Voice (creator's words, adapt freely) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:05 | **Hook** | Creator on camera | "Every call I run ends the same way: so... who's doing what?" |
| 0:05 to 0:15 | **Problem** | Stale task board, unread notes | "On a small team nobody's job is follow-ups. So I chase them, or nobody does, and by Friday the tracker is out of date." |
| 0:15 to 0:30 | **Octo on a call** | Screen recording of a Meet, Zoom or Teams call with Octo's tile | "This is Octo. It joins the calls I choose from my calendar. Before the call it sent me a brief: who's coming, what we discussed last time, what's still open." |
| 0:30 to 0:42 | **The "just talk" moment** | Creator says "Octo, where are we on the launch?"; answer appears on Octo's tile *(see note)*. Then cut to after the call: DMs with tasks, the Notion or Asana card updating | "We just talk. When I ask, Octo answers on its tile. After the call each person gets their own tasks by DM, with a deadline and the exact quote, and my tracker, CRM and Notion are already updated." |
| 0:42 to 0:55 | **CTA** | Link on screen, pinned comment | "It works like the project manager my team doesn't have yet. Start a free trial with my link below. Their team sets it up on your calls." |

Note on the 0:30 beat: the brief marks "speaks when called by name" as *to confirm* (the stage demo notes say it answers everything it hears). Until Oleksii confirms, keep the question but drop any claim that it stays silent otherwise.

On-screen end card: "Just talk. Octo keeps your tracker, CRM and Notion up to date. Start free trial."

### Brief for creators: what you must not claim

> Thanks for trying Octo. Say what you saw on your own calls, in your own words. Please do not say that Octo is self-serve or that viewers can sign up and start alone: our team sets it up. Don't say it is invisible to guests, that it translates calls, or that it has a sales copilot panel (agenda, what to ask, sentiment); none of these exist. Don't say it only joins when invited: you choose which calendar events it joins, and it joins them on its own. Don't say it plans or prioritises work: it tracks what the team agrees. Don't promise fast answers to deep questions (they take 20 to 60 seconds) or full features when an outside guest joins (it switches to a reduced client mode). Don't quote a price, customer numbers, call counts or hours saved, or name specific CRM brands ("your CRM" is fine). Don't call it a "bot", an "AI teammate" or an "AI meeting agent" in your title or thumbnail. A few rare screens still show Ukrainian labels; skip them or mention it. Mark the video as paid promotion in YouTube when we pay you.
